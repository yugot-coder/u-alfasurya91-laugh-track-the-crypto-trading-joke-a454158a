# Candidates — Laugh Track: The Crypto Trading Joke Contest

One line, max 200 characters, about crypto and trading life. The judge reads
entries two at a time and weighs, in order: (1) a clear setup and turn,
(2) a real laugh with a surprise payoff, (3) a recognisable trading truth
(the chart, the bag, the group chat), (4) tight phrasing with the punchline
at the end, (5) repeatable to a friend. All candidates are original lines
written for this entry.

## 1. "I bought the dip. Turns out the dip had a basement, a sub-basement, and a parking garage."
**CHOSEN.** Setup ("bought the dip") every trader knows, then the turn is
pure escalation: the dip is not a floor, it is a building going down.
Specific trading truth (the dip keeps dipping), punchline lands on the last
words, 87 characters, zero throat-clearing, and a trader would repeat it to
a friend after the next red candle.

## 2. "My stop-loss triggers at the exact price where the coin immediately reverses and moons without me."
Strong and brutally true — every trader has been wicked out seconds before
the pump. Weakness: it is a complaint more than a joke; the turn is implied
rather than surprising, and it reads slightly bitter instead of funny.

## 3. "I checked my portfolio at 3am and the chart was so red my phone asked if I was okay."
Good image (the 3am chart check is a real trading ritual) and a funny
personification. Weakness: the punchline is the phone caring, which is a
generic "device judges me" gag that could fit any bad habit, not trading.

## 4. "I used 100x leverage to finally afford a house — the bank owns it now, and I live in the liquidation notice."
Specific (leverage, liquidation) and the turn is sharp. Weakness: two
clauses make it wordy, and "live in the liquidation notice" needs a beat
to parse — the punchline is clever rather than instantly funny.

## 5. "I sold at the bottom to buy back higher — I'm not trading, I'm donating to the market with extra steps."
True and self-aware; "donating with extra steps" is a good turn.
Weakness: the setup is a confession, not a scene, and the payoff is a
label rather than an image — it explains the joke instead of landing it.

## 6. "My group chat said HODL, so I held all the way down and now my bags need their own seat on the plane."
Recognisable detail (the group chat, the bags). Weakness: the seat-on-the-
plane image is cute but soft; the laugh is a smile, not a turn, and "bags"
needs the reader to already know the slang.

## 7. "I told my wife I'm a crypto trader. She thinks I'm a gambler with a spreadsheet."
Clean setup and turn, and the spreadsheet detail is true. Weakness: it is
about how trading looks from outside, not about trading life itself, and
the "gambler" punchline is the oldest crypto joke there is.

## 8. "My portfolio is down 90% but my conviction is up 100% — that's how math works in crypto."
The contradiction is funny and the copium is real. Weakness: it is a
one-liner about a ratio, with no scene and no surprise — the reader sees
the punchline coming from the first half.

## 9. "My crypto portfolio is like my gym membership — I pay every month and never see any gains."
Relatable and instantly understood. Weakness: it is a generic
"X is like Y" comparison that could be written about any subscription;
nothing in it is specific to trading life, and there is no turn.

## 10. "The exchange sent me a push notification: 'Congratulations, you've been liquidated!' — the only win I've had all year."
Dark and specific (liquidation, push notification). Weakness: it needs
quotation marks and two voices, which eat characters and slow the read;
the punchline ("only win") is a sigh, not a laugh.

## Verdict
Candidate 1 wins: the tightest setup-to-payoff ratio, the most specific
trading truth, the clearest surprise, and the most repeatable line.
